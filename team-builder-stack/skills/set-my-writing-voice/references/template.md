# The writing voice skill template

Fill every slot marked with angle brackets. Save the result as a skill named `writing-voice`, or the name the person chooses.

```markdown
---
name: writing-voice
description: "Follow whenever you write anything a person will read; its source is <name>'s own writing."
---

# Writing voice

**Type:** standard skill.

Written with Set my writing voice on <date>, from the person's own writing. When their writing or corrections no longer match it, say so and suggest Set my writing voice.

## Tone

<two or three sentences on how the writing should feel. Start from plain words, short sentences, and technical detail only when asked, and change that only where the person's own writing says otherwise>

## Words to use

<the person's words for their work, and phrases they like>

## Words to avoid

<words and habits to cut, each with what to say instead>

## Format for each place

| Place | Format |
| --- | --- |
| <a chat> | <length, structure> |
| <an email> | <length, structure, sign-off> |
| <a record page> | <headings, what goes first> |

## Examples of the real thing

<two or three pieces of the person's own writing, with names swapped for made-up ones>

## Steps

1. Before you write, read the format for the place you are writing to.
2. Draft. Lead with the news or the ask.
3. Check the draft against Words to avoid and the format. Fix what fails.
4. Compare it with the examples. If it wouldn't sit next to them, rewrite it.

## What good looks like

- It reads like the examples.
- It's plain and short: “Account B’s renewal moved to April. I updated the page.” is the whole message.
- Nothing from Words to avoid.
- The first sentence says the point.

## Weekly refresh

Compare drafts with what the person sent, and propose changes. Change nothing until they say yes.
```
