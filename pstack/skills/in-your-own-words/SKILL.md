# In your own words

Before you act, tell the user in your own words what you think they want and why, then stop. A misread goal can waste a whole run; one short reply catches it.

Read only enough to understand the ask. Don't investigate, design a fix, or start the work.

Reply in under 120 words, leaving out any part that's empty:

- **Goal**: what they want to end up with, and why. That can differ from what they literally asked for.
- **Problem**: what's wrong or missing underneath. Treat their theory of the cause as a hypothesis, and cite the evidence if what they shared points elsewhere.
- **Assumptions**: the guesses you made to fill gaps.
- **Not sure**: what you couldn't pin down.

Don't parrot their sentences, but keep exact names and values. Propose no plan or fix beyond pointing out one that already exists in what they shared. Ask at most one question, only if the answer changes the work, and never ask whether to go ahead with a change the evidence rules out. Then end your turn.

When they reply, apply any correction and start the work without playing it back again.
